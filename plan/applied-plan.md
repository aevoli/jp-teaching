## KS1 — Year 1 & 2 (Ages 5–7)

### Lesson 1: Animal Names & Sounds

**App page:** Animal Sounds
**Learning objective:** Say 4–6 animal names in Japanese; recognise the Japanese animal sound
**Resources:** App (Animal Sounds page), space to stand up

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------- | ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 2 min | Teacher makes an animal sound (e.g. "Wan wan!") — _"What animal is that? In Japan, dogs say wan wan not woof!"_                                                               |
| Intro      | 5 min | Open **Animal Sounds → Learn Animal Names**. Teacher taps each animal, class repeats the Japanese name together. Do 4–6 animals only — don't overload.                        |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | **Flip Cards** tab. Teacher taps a card (green = hiragana). Ask: _"Can anyone guess what this says?"_ Tap to reveal romaji, then emoji. Class calls out the English together. |
| Activity 2 | 8 min | **Simon Says in Japanese** — teacher calls out an animal in Japanese (_"Inu!"_), children mime the animal's English sound. Swap: children call out, teacher mimes.            |
| Summary    | 3 min | Tap 3 random animals on the learn tab. Class whispers the name, then shouts it. Thumbs up/down: _"Did we learn something new today?"_                                         |

#### 💡 First-Time Teacher Tips

- **Don't worry about perfect pronunciation.** Model it, let children attempt it, celebrate effort. Japanese pronunciation is very consistent — what you see is what you say.
- **Tap the app yourself on the board** rather than letting children run up individually — it keeps pace and avoids chaos at KS1.
- **Use the emoji as a scaffold.** If a child can't remember the Japanese word, pointing to the emoji and asking _"what's the Japanese for this?"_ is a low-stakes prompt.
- **Repeat, repeat, repeat.** KS1 needs to hear a word 7–10 times before it sticks. The flip card cycle (tap → romaji → emoji) gives natural repetition.

#### ⚠️ Contingencies

- **Children too excited / won't settle:** Use a call-and-response — say _"Minna!"_ (everyone), they respond _"Hai!"_ (yes). Practise this at the start of every lesson until it's automatic.
- **Flip cards too hard:** Stay on the Learn tab only. Tap each animal, repeat as a class, move on.
- **Lesson running short:** Add a "freeze" game — play audio of a Japanese animal name, children freeze in the shape of that animal.
- **Lesson running long:** Cut Activity 2. The app activity is the core learning.

### Lesson 2: Colours

**App page:** Colour Hunt
**Learning objective:** Name 4–6 colours in Japanese; respond to _"Nani iro?"_ (What colour?)

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 2 min | Hold up a coloured object. _"In Japanese, red is… aka!"_ Ask children to look around the room for something red.                                          |
| Intro      | 6 min | Open **Colour Hunt**. Spin the wheel together 3–4 times. Each time it lands, class repeats the colour name. Point to something in the room that colour.   |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | Teacher calls a colour in Japanese. Children race to touch something that colour in the room and sit back down. First 3 back get a point for their table. |
| Activity 2 | 7 min | Spin the wheel again — this time children try to say the colour _before_ the result card appears. Celebrate when they get it right.                       |
| Summary    | 3 min | Teacher points to 4 colours on the grid at the bottom of the app. Class calls out the Japanese. _"Which colour was hardest? Which was easiest?"_          |

#### 💡 Tips

- **Shiro (white)** has a gray border on the app so it's visible — point this out: _"White in Japanese is shiro — tricky to see on a white board!"_
- **Aka (red) and ao (blue)** are the easiest to remember — anchor harder ones to these.
- **The room is your resource.** Primary classrooms are full of colour — use displays, clothing, books.

#### ⚠️ Contingencies

- **Children keep shouting random colours:** Give each table a colour "team name" in Japanese. They can only shout when their colour comes up.
- **Wheel lands on the same colour twice:** Use it — _"We've seen this one before — who remembers?"_

### Lesson 3: Greetings

**App page:** Greetings Karaoke
**Learning objective:** Use 3–4 Japanese greetings appropriately; understand time-of-day context

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 2 min | Walk in and say _"Ohayou!"_ to the class. Look confused when they don't respond. _"In Japan, when someone says ohayou, you say… ohayou back!"_                      |
| Intro      | 6 min | **Greetings → Learn tab.** Go through ohayou, konnichiwa, konbanwa, oyasumi. For each: hear it, repeat it, discuss when you'd use it.                               |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | **Match tab.** Teacher does it on the board as a class activity — read each romaji chip aloud together, then drag it to the English. Cheer for each correct match.  |
| Activity 2 | 7 min | **Greeting walk** — children walk around the room. Teacher calls a time of day (_"It's morning!"_). Children greet each person they meet with the correct greeting. |
| Summary    | 3 min | _"If I arrive at school at 8am, what do I say? If I see a friend at lunchtime?"_ Quick-fire questions. End with everyone saying _"Mata ne!"_ (See you!)             |

#### 💡 Tips

- **Context is everything with greetings.** Children remember _when_ to use a word better than the word itself — always pair the greeting with a time or situation.
- **Mata ne** is a great lesson closer — use it every single lesson as a routine. By week 3 they'll say it unprompted.
- **The Match tab works well on a whiteboard** — drag and drop is intuitive for this age with teacher modelling first.

## KS2 Lower — Year 3 & 4 (Ages 7–9)

### Lesson 1: Numbers 1–10

**App page:** Number Ninja
**Learning objective:** Count to 10 in Japanese; use numbers in a game context

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time   | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 2 min  | _"How do you count to 3 in French? Spanish? What about Japanese?"_ Take guesses. _"Today we're learning ichi, ni, san…"_                                                                                                  |
| Intro      | 6 min  | **Number Ninja → Learn Numbers.** Tap each number — class repeats. Focus on the kanji too: _"This is how Japanese people write the number — it's actually a picture of the number!"_                                      |
| Activity 1 | 10 min | **Ninja Game** — split class into Team 1 and Team 2. Use the Hajime button. When the number appears, first team to hold up that many fingers AND say the Japanese word wins the round. Teacher awards points via the app. |
| Activity 2 | 6 min  | **Buzz** — count around the class in Japanese. Every time you hit a multiple of 3 (san, roku, kyuu), say _"Ninja!"_ instead.                                                                                              |
| Summary    | 3 min  | Teacher says a number in Japanese — children write it on mini whiteboards (digit, not word). _"What's the Japanese for 7? For 4?"_                                                                                        |

#### 💡 Tips

- **Yon, nana, kyuu** are the only versions in the app — this is deliberate. Shi (4) sounds like "death" in Japanese and is considered unlucky; nana and kyuu are the everyday counting forms. Mention this — children love cultural facts.
- **The countdown animation** (3-2-1 before Hajime) builds genuine excitement. Let it play out — don't rush past it.
- **Kanji for numbers are genuinely logical** — 一 is one line, 二 is two lines, 三 is three lines. Point this out. It makes the writing system feel accessible.

#### ⚠️ Contingencies

- **Teams get competitive/argumentative:** Award points for _both_ saying the word AND holding up fingers — this slows it down and makes it fairer.
- **Children finish early:** Challenge: can they count backwards from 10? Can they do it in under 10 seconds?
- **App audio not working:** Use your own voice. The game still works — teacher says the number, teams compete.

### Lesson 2: Body Parts (HSKT)

**App page:** Heads, Shoulders, Knees & Toes
**Learning objective:** Name 4–8 body parts in Japanese; perform the HSKT song in Japanese

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 2 min | Play/hum the HSKT tune. _"You all know this song — but do you know it in Japanese?"_                                                                      |
| Intro      | 6 min | **HSKT → English Version tab.** Show the 4 cards (head/shoulders/knees/toes). Tap each — class repeats Japanese AND touches that body part.               |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | **Japanese Version tab.** All 8 body parts. Tap through them. Then: teacher taps a card but covers the English — class must say the Japanese from memory. |
| Activity 2 | 8 min | **Dance Mode** — do the song together. Start slow, then speed up. Try it with eyes closed for the last round.                                             |
| Summary    | 3 min | _"Point to your hiza! Your mimi! Your hana!"_ Quick-fire. _"Which body part was hardest to remember?"_                                                    |

#### 💡 Tips

- **Physical movement dramatically improves retention** at this age. The act of touching the body part while saying the word creates a kinaesthetic memory anchor.
- **Speed rounds** are highly motivating — children will beg to do it faster.
- **Eyes/ears/mouth/nose** are harder because they don't appear in the English version of the song. Treat them as a bonus challenge for children who are ready.

### Lesson 3: Food Market

**App page:** Food Market
**Learning objective:** Name 6+ Japanese foods; use _〇〇をください_ to order food

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Hook       | 3 min | Show the food emojis on the Menu tab. _"Which of these have you tried? Which looks most/least appetising?"_                                                                                            |
| Intro      | 5 min | Tap through the Menu. For each food: class reads the romaji, teacher explains the description (_"Yakitori — grilled chicken on a skewer, like a Japanese kebab"_).                                     |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | **Role Play Phrases tab.** Teach _irasshaimase_ (shopkeeper) and _〇〇をください_ (customer). Pairs: one is shopkeeper, one is customer. Customer taps a food on the Menu tab and must say the phrase. |
| Activity 2 | 8 min | **Ordering Game tab.** Do the first 2–3 problems as a class on the board. Then let pairs take turns dragging.                                                                                          |
| Summary    | 3 min | _"If you were in Japan and wanted sushi, what would you say?"_ Cold-call 3–4 children. End with _"Arigatou gozaimasu!"_ — class responds _"Douzo!"_                                                    |

#### 💡 Tips

- **The ordering game** is the highlight — children genuinely enjoy the drag mechanic. Circulate and ask _"Why did you choose that one?"_ to push thinking.
- **Cultural context sells it.** Mention that in Japan, the shopkeeper always says _irasshaimase_ when you walk in — it's automatic, like a reflex. Children find this fascinating.
- **Yakitori and matcha** are the most culturally rich items — spend an extra 30 seconds on each.

## KS2 Upper — Year 5 & 6 (Ages 9–11)

### Lesson 1: Hiragana Introduction

**App page:** Hiragana Detective
**Learning objective:** Recognise and read 10+ hiragana characters; decode a simple word

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Hook       | 3 min | Write _ねこ_ on the board. _"This is a Japanese word written in hiragana. Can anyone guess what it means?"_ (It means cat.)                                                                                                          |
| Intro      | 7 min | **Hiragana Detective → Learn Hiragana.** Tap through the first row (a, i, u, e, o) and the ka row. For each: class repeats. Discuss: _"Unlike English, every hiragana character has exactly one sound — no exceptions."_             |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | **Crack the Code tab.** Do message #1 (sushi) together as a class. Teacher models: _"I need to find す — that's 'su'. Then し — that's 'shi'. What word is that?"_ The text hint shows automatically; emoji hint available if stuck. |
| Activity 2 | 7 min | Children attempt messages #2 and #3 independently or in pairs. Teacher circulates.                                                                                                                                                   |
| Summary    | 3 min | _"What's the difference between hiragana and the alphabet?"_ (Each character = one syllable, not one letter-sound.) Write 3 hiragana on the board — class reads them.                                                                |

#### 💡 Tips

- **The "one sound per character" rule** is the most important concept. English children are used to letters that change sound depending on context (c in _cat_ vs _city_). Hiragana has no exceptions — framing this as _easier_ builds confidence.
- **Don't try to teach all 46 characters in one lesson.** Five characters done well beats 46 characters half-remembered.
- **The secret message mechanic** is genuinely motivating for this age — it feels like a puzzle, not a language lesson.

#### ⚠️ Contingencies

- **Children find it too hard:** Stay on the learn tab. Tap each character, repeat, move on. The decode activity can wait for lesson 2.
- **Children find it too easy:** Ask them to write the hiragana characters from memory after seeing them once. Can they write _ねこ_ (neko) without looking?
- **No audio:** The characters are still readable — the lesson works without sound, just use your voice.

### Lesson 2: Self-Introduction (Jikoshoukai)

**App page:** Jikoshoukai Builder
**Learning objective:** Introduce themselves in Japanese using name, age, nationality, and an interest

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 2 min | Teacher gives a full self-introduction in Japanese: _"Watashi wa [name] desu. [age]-sai desu. Igirisu-jin desu. [interest] ga suki desu."_ Ask: _"How much did you understand?"_ |
| Intro      | 6 min | **Jikoshoukai Builder.** Walk through each section: name → age → nationality → interest. Tap each option to hear it. Explain the grammar pattern: _[topic] wa [info] desu._      |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | Children build their own introduction using the app. Teacher circulates — ask each child to say their introduction aloud quietly.                                                |
| Activity 2 | 8 min | **Jikoshoukai circle** — children stand. Each child says one sentence of their introduction to the person next to them. Rotate. Aim for 3 rotations.                             |
| Summary    | 3 min | Cold-call 3 children to give their full introduction to the class. Class listens and writes down one thing they understood.                                                      |

#### 💡 Tips

- **Personalisation is the key motivator at this age.** The moment children are talking about _themselves_, engagement spikes. Don't skip the circle activity.
- **The grammar pattern** (_wa … desu_) is worth writing on the board as a sentence frame. Y5/6 can handle this level of explicit grammar.
- **Nationality vocabulary** — many country names in Japanese are phonetic approximations of English: Igirisu (England), Amerikajin (American). This demystifies the language.

#### ⚠️ Contingencies

- **Children are shy about speaking aloud:** Start with whispering to a partner, then build to the circle. Never force a child to speak to the whole class cold.
- **Children want to say something the app doesn't have:** Write it on the board in romaji and praise the initiative. _"You've just gone beyond the lesson — that's brilliant."_

### Lesson 3: Kanji — Origins and Quiz

**App page:** Kanji Evolution
**Learning objective:** Recognise 5+ kanji; understand that kanji are pictographic in origin

#### Timing

| Phase      | Time  | What happens                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 3 min | Draw a rough mountain shape on the board. _"What does this look like?"_ Then show 山 (yama). _"This is the kanji for mountain — it literally looks like one."_                            |
| Intro      | 7 min | **Kanji Evolution → Explore tab.** Click through each kanji. For each: show the pictograph description, read the meaning and hiragana. Ask: _"Can you see the picture in the character?"_ |
| Activity 1 | 8 min | **Quiz tab.** Do the first 3 questions as a class — discuss the options before answering. After each answer, read the hiragana aloud together.                                            |
| Activity 2 | 7 min | Children continue the quiz independently or in pairs. Extension: _"Can you draw any kanji from memory? Design a new kanji for 'school' — what would it look like?"_                       |
| Summary    | 3 min | Teacher shows 5 kanji on the board. Class writes the English meaning. Discuss: _"Why do you think Japan has 3 different writing systems?"_                                                |

#### 💡 Tips

- **The pictographic origin story** is the single most powerful hook for this age group. The idea that writing started as drawings — and that you can still see the picture in the character — genuinely changes how children perceive Japanese.
- **The hiragana reading shown after each quiz answer** reinforces the connection between kanji and sound. Point this out explicitly: _"The kanji tells you the meaning; the hiragana tells you how to say it."_
- **"Design your own kanji"** is a brilliant extension — it demonstrates understanding of the concept and produces great display work.

#### ⚠️ Contingencies

- **Quiz is too hard:** Use Explore mode only. Children can still learn all 7 kanji without the quiz pressure.
- **Quiz is too easy:** Ask children to write the hiragana reading from memory _before_ the answer is revealed.
- **Great discussion question if time:** _"English used to have pictographic writing too — Egyptian hieroglyphics influenced early writing. Why did English change but Japanese kept kanji?"_

## General Advice for First-Time Teachers

### Classroom Management

- **Establish a Japanese call-and-response from lesson 1.** _"Minna!"_ → _"Hai!"_ works perfectly. It's culturally authentic and gives you instant attention without raising your voice.
- **Always model before asking children to do.** Tap the app yourself first, repeat the word yourself first. Children need to see it's safe to try.
- **Celebrate approximation.** A child who says _"yama"_ with a strong English accent is doing brilliantly. Correct gently and privately, never publicly.

### Pacing

- **30 minutes goes fast.** If you're running behind, cut Activity 2 — never cut the Summary. The summary is where learning consolidates.
- **If you finish early,** the app has enough content to extend any activity. The Flip Cards, Ordering Game, and Quiz all have natural replay value.
- **Transitions between app tabs** can lose 1–2 minutes if you're not prepared. Know which tab you're going to before the lesson starts.

### Using the App

- **Project on a whiteboard** for whole-class phases. Let children use it on a device (tablet/laptop) for independent/pair phases if available.
- **Audio is central** — test your speakers before every lesson. If audio fails, use your own voice; the visual content still works.
- **The app works offline** — no internet dependency during lessons.

### Cultural Framing

- **Always give a "why Japan?" moment** in each lesson. A quick fact (dogs say wan wan, kanji come from pictures, Japan has 3 writing systems) makes the language feel real and worth learning.
- **Avoid treating Japanese as "exotic."** Frame it as a language like any other — logical, learnable, and spoken by 125 million people.

### Assessment

- **Informal assessment every lesson:** cold-call questions in the summary, mini whiteboard responses, thumbs up/middle/down for confidence.
- **Progression markers:** KS1 → can say words; KS2 Lower → can use words in a phrase; KS2 Upper → can construct a sentence.
- **The app activities are themselves assessment tools** — the quiz scores, ordering game completion, and match tab all show you who has understood.
